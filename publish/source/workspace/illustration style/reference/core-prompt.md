# Canonical Chapter-Art Core Prompt

Copy this block unchanged into every chapter-image generation request. Follow it
with a scene-specific brief and only the reference entries needed for that
scene.

```text
Create a highly detailed fantasy adventure-book illustration drawn entirely in black ink on clean white paper.

VISUAL LANGUAGE
Traditional hand-drawn dip-pen artwork with visible pen strokes, natural wobble, uneven line weight and occasional construction or sketch lines. Use expressive contours, loose feathering, fine cross-hatching and stippling, balanced by deliberate white space and clear silhouettes. The result should feel like an original early-1980s British fantasy gamebook interior plate drawn by hand at the table: atmospheric, characterful and slightly imperfect, never digitally polished.

Render materials through linework. Stone grain, worn leather, fur, metal, scales, cloth, dust, water and shadow should each have distinct hand-inked textures. Use solid areas of black selectively for depth and emphasis. Detail must support the scene rather than overwhelm it.

COMPOSITION
Follow the supplied scene-specific brief for the moment, location, cast, mood, pace, viewpoint, framing, orientation and dominant focus. Compose around one clear visual idea. Include only characters and creatures requested by the scene brief. Do not automatically show the whole adventuring party. Quiet, ominous, intimate, spacious, environmental and creature-focused images are as valid as action scenes. Avoid crowded ensemble staging unless the scene specifically calls for it.

REFERENCE DISCIPLINE
Use each attached image only for the role assigned to it:
- style references define pen technique, density and visual mood;
- character references define identity, proportions, face, species, clothing and signature equipment;
- creature references define anatomy, body plan, scale and distinctive physical features;
- location references define architecture, construction, materials and spatial character;
- object references define the form and details of a specific prop.

Do not copy the composition, background, colour palette or rendering style of an anatomy, character or location reference unless the scene brief explicitly requests that. Translate every reference into this established monochrome hand-inked visual language. Written scene requirements take priority over incidental details in reference images.

TECHNICAL CONSTRAINTS
Pure black linework on a clean white background only. No colour or coloured accents. No greyscale wash, soft shading, airbrush, digital painting, photorealism, vector-clean lines, engraving simulation, parchment texture, artificial outer border, caption, lettering, signature or watermark. Do not add cave formations such as stalactites inside intentionally built stone halls unless the scene brief requests them.
```

## Core composition principles

- Prefer one dominant subject, action or emotional beat.
- Include the entire party only when the scene genuinely needs an ensemble.
- Let the scene determine orientation: landscape, portrait, square and narrow
  spot illustrations are all valid.
- Match composition to pace. Use space and restraint for suspense, quiet or
  wonder; use stronger diagonals, compression and black shapes for speed or
  violence.
- Establish scale with figures or architecture on a comparable depth plane.
- Make every visible limb, weapon and shield trace clearly back to its owner.
- Keep silhouettes separated; do not merge figures into monsters, pillars,
  walls or one another.
